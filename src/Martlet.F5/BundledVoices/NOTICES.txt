Martlet bundled F5 reference voices - sources and notices
==========================================================

Martlet includes ten short reference recordings (src/Martlet.F5/BundledVoices) that
F5 can copy a voice from. Each is public domain or covered by the CMU ARCTIC licence
below, which allows any use, including commercial use and redistribution. They were
cut from the upstream recordings named here by scripts/Build-F5BundledVoices.py.

Modifications (all ten clips): the spoken passage was cut out at its silences (the two
CMU ARCTIC sentences joined by 0.3 s of silence), DC offset and rumble below 50 Hz were
removed, the level was set to -20 dBFS speech RMS (peaks at most -1 dBFS), 10 ms fades
were applied at each cut, LibriVox MP3s were decoded and resampled to 24 kHz, and each
clip was written as mono 16-bit PCM WAV. The words and voices are otherwise unchanged.

These licences cover the recordings. Speech Martlet generates in these voices is not
said or endorsed by the original speakers.


Public domain recordings
------------------------

lj-speech.wav ("LJ (female narrator)")
  The LJ Speech Dataset 1.1 (https://keithito.com/LJ-Speech-Dataset/), wavs/LJ001-0009.wav
  (SHA-256 d003373f9c769b17995cfff2a99818e720a56f3376678b27fc6c9c27539c3f75), from
  LJSpeech-1.1.tar.bz2 (SHA-256 be1a30453f28eb8dd26af4101ae40cbf2c50413b1bb21936cbcdc6fae3de8aa5).
  "The texts ... are in the public domain. The audio was recorded in 2016-17 by the
  LibriVox project and is also in the public domain."
  Transcript: "Printing, then, for our purpose, may be considered as the art of making
  books by means of movable types."

librivox-cori-samuel.wav ("Cori (female narrator)")
  LibriVox recording of Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus (1818) by Mary Wollstonecraft
  Shelley, read by Cori Samuel (https://archive.org/details/frankenstein_cs_librivox,
  dedicated to the public domain, CC0 1.0), frankenstein_01_shelley.mp3
  (SHA-256 96068a62c2549a698a35e986c8425368d0fbd4ad757bad9a5b5690c4e2b4b951), 105.24-113.87 s.
  Transcript: "I have thus endeavoured to preserve the truth of the elementary principles
  of human nature, while I have not scrupled to innovate upon their combinations."

librivox-helen-taylor.wav ("Helen (female narrator)")
  LibriVox recording of Love at Second Sight by Ada Leverson, read by Helen Taylor
  (https://archive.org/details/lovesecondsight_1604_librivox, public domain mark 1.0),
  secondsight_01_leverson.mp3
  (SHA-256 b49193791e6090b0623caeac494c6f57596a9ef0c1d9d4f621de97286480898a), 110.88-117.93 s.
  Transcript: "She was a slim, fair, pretty woman, with more vividness and character than
  usually goes with her type."

Institute, Carnegie Mellon University. Each clip joins arctic_a0058.wav and
arctic_a0039.wav of one speaker:
